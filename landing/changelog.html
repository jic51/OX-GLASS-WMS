v12.49 Fix

What you just saved now appears the second the server confirms it

Saving ten deliveries, timed frame by frame: the window closed after 14 seconds and the movement list took a further 16 and a half to show them. Sixteen seconds looking at a table that does not contain what you just entered is exactly when somebody decides it failed and presses save again.

It was not slow. The browser was asking for the whole picture again to learn about rows the server had just written and was already handing back. Those rows now go straight into the list, with their own ids so the quiet refresh behind them cannot show anything twice.

Opening this up turned up something bigger: the live stock figures the server has calculated since v12.24 — so the screen would not have to ask — were never being passed on by any of the three save paths. The browser asked for them and got nothing. Fixed in all three, with a test that now pins the whole return trip.

v12.48 Fix

A typo in an email address could only be fixed in the spreadsheet

An address registered with one letter wrong — @ox-glasss.com instead of @ox-glass.com — is a perfectly valid email, so nothing refused it. It sat in the user list looking active, and simply never let that person in. And the email field could not be edited, so correcting one letter meant opening the Google Sheet by hand.

An administrator can now correct an address in the app. Three guards sit behind it: an address already registered to someone else is refused, nobody can change their own address (they would lose access the instant it saved), and the change is recorded from-and-to in the audit log.

And before saving, an address whose domain is one or two letters away from one everybody else already uses now asks whether you meant the other one. Close-but-different is exactly what a typo looks like; a genuinely different domain — a contractor on Gmail — says nothing.

v12.48 Fix

The nightly backup can stop without anybody noticing

The switch stays green, the list stays full, the newest copy still has its date and a link that opens. Nothing said that date was from last week. A backup system that fails in silence is worse than having none, because anyone with none knows it.

Settings → System now says it outright when the nightly copy has not run for more than a day and a half, names the most common cause, and points at Back up now. It stays quiet when the backup is switched off on purpose, and it does not cry wolf over a single late night.

v12.47 Fix

“Google rejected that key” when Google had rejected nothing

Saving an AI key while Google’s service was busy produced “Google rejected that key (HTTP 503)”. A 503 means Google is overloaded. It does not look at the key at all — so a perfectly good key was reported as bad, and the obvious response is to go and make another one, and another.

A busy Google is no longer treated as a verdict on your key. The key is saved, and the app says plainly that it could not be checked right now and why. Only the errors that really are about the key — copied incompletely, or the Generative Language API switched off — still refuse it, and now they say which.

Checking a key also stopped trying four models in a row. That fallback list exists so the reader keeps working when Google retires a model; it proves nothing about a key, and it made the wait four times longer than it needed to be.

v12.46 Fix

Renaming: one at a time, and nothing you typed gets thrown away

In Settings, pressing the pencil on several categories opened them all for editing — but saving one repaints the list and threw away whatever you had typed in the others, with no warning. The app was offering work it could not deliver.

Now one rename is open at a time. If the open one has unsaved text, the new one does not open and the app says why — your typing is never discarded to make room. If it is untouched, it just closes and the next one opens, because pressing the wrong pencil and correcting yourself is the most common thing there is.

It saves one at a time on purpose: renaming a category rewrites every movement that used it and then rebuilds the stock cache. Five at once is five of those passes, and if the third fails you are left with half a batch applied and no way to tell which half.

v12.45 Security

Two guards that said yes by default

Who may record movements is now a list of who can, not a list of who cannot. The app used to ask "is this person a VIEWER?" instead of "may this person write?". With three roles those give the same answer — which is why it never showed. But any new role would have walked straight through, simply by not being called VIEWER, and in a warehouse that means permission to move material. Nothing could do that today; it would have been true the day after a fourth role existed.

And a category that is not in your catalog can no longer come in with material. A category is half of a material's identity here — file something under WINDOWS when the catalog says WINDOW and it is not the same material badly labelled, it is a second material with its own stock that nobody ever adds up.

You could never type one: the category box is a closed dropdown. What could get through was everything that does not go through the screen — an import, mainly. Imports still bring their categories in, because an import means "this is my warehouse as it stands", but now they are written into your catalog and counted. That matters more than it sounds: a material whose category is not in the list cannot even be selected to take it out again.

Taking material OUT is never blocked by either check — whatever is already inside has to be able to leave.

v12.44 Fix

The daily report was telling you about a day that had not happened

Three things were wrong at once, and together they meant the movements of a working day were never reported at all. The email arrived, looked fine, and always said nothing had happened.

It went out at the wrong hour. The app wrote down the hour it had scheduled the job for, and later trusted that note instead of the job itself — and Google gives no way to ask a scheduled job what time it runs, so the note could never be checked. Once the two drifted apart, Settings said 4:00 PM and the email came at 2:53 AM. Now the job wakes up every hour and the app decides when it is time, so there is nothing left that can drift. Changing the time in Settings takes effect without touching anything.

And it always reported "today", whenever it ran. At 2 AM, "today" is three hours old and empty. The rule now: a morning email reports the day that ended; an evening one reports the day that is ending. The email still names the day it covers, so a quiet day reads as a quiet day instead of as a lie.

The date is written the US way. October 6th was printing as 06/10/2026, which reads as June 10th.

Separately: 🏭 Acopio → Check installation now fills in missing column names on your tabs. The code to do it existed and nothing ever called it outside first-time setup, so an installation that was already running could never get it — which is why RESERVATIONS and AUDIT_LOG had blank headers. A column with no name looks like junk, and sooner or later somebody deletes it.

v12.43 Fix

Material can no longer be put in a rack that does not exist

Typing a rack that is not in your warehouse — A1P when you meant A1B — used to save it anyway, and a brand-new location appeared on your warehouse map with material in it. That is worse than a typo: an invented rack is not on any shelf, so the app says the material is somewhere nobody can go to.

Now the app checks the rack the material is arriving at, and if it is not one of yours it says so — and offers the one you probably meant. If you can manage the catalog, you can create it right there and the movement saves without retyping it.

Taking material OUT of a rack that does not exist is still allowed, on purpose. Whatever ended up in A1P before this fix has to be able to come out, or the fix would trap it there for good. So the rack list you pick from when material leaves still shows everywhere stock actually is; the one for where it goes shows only real locations.

Importing a file still creates the racks it names — importing means "this is my warehouse as it stands" — but it adds them to your location list and tells you how many, instead of creating them quietly.

v12.42 Easier

The link to the app is a link now — click it

Publishing an update used to hand you the address as plain text in a narrow box, cut off, with a scrollbar. To use it you had to drag-select it blind, copy it, switch to the browser and paste. That is because the alert box Google gives us can only show text — it cannot show a link.

Now both 🔧 Advanced → Push Update Live and Open App show a proper window with an Open button that goes straight there, the full address in a field you can select, and a Copy button that only says Copied when the copy actually worked.

New people get the link by email. Adding someone in Manage Users now sends them an invitation with the address, which Google account to sign in with, and what their role lets them do. Nobody has to find the address and forward it by hand. You can untick that if you are setting accounts up in advance — and if the email cannot be sent, the app says so instead of claiming it went.

Bookmark that address once and the spreadsheet stops being part of anybody's day.

v12.41 Security

One user list, not two

Installations upgraded from an older version carried two lists of users: the one you manage in Manage Users, and an older one still sitting in the CONFIG tab. Both let people in — but only one of them is on your screen. So anybody who was in the old list could sign in without ever appearing in Manage Users, which means you had no way to stop them from inside the app.

The old list is not deleted: in a half-migrated installation the people in it are real staff, and removing it overnight would lock somebody out of their job on a Tuesday morning. Instead it has become a one-way door. The next time one of those people signs in, they are written into your real user list on the spot — same role, still active, nothing changed about their access — and from that moment they appear in Manage Users with their own switch, so you can turn them off.

🏭 Acopio → Check installation now does the whole crossing at once instead of waiting for people to sign in. It tells you how many were brought across, which emails carry a different role in each list (Manage Users is the one that counts), and which rows of your user list have something other than an email where the email goes — those grant nothing, but they show up on screen. It never deletes a row.

v12.40 Security

Refused sign-ins are now written down — and you get told

The door worked: somebody who is not on your user list was turned away. But the refusal left no trace anywhere, so from the inside an attempt to get in and a quiet day looked exactly the same. That is a lock without a peephole.

Every refusal now lands in the AUDIT_LOG tab of your spreadsheet under ACCESS_DENIED, with who tried and why. One line per person per minute — a forgotten browser tab retries on its own every twenty seconds, and without that cap a single abandoned screen would bury the one line that mattered.

And at ten refusals in ten minutes you get an email, at most one an hour: who tried, what to do if it is one of your own people, and where the full record is. A log nobody opens warns nobody.

Being refused permission for one action — a read-only user pressing save — is not recorded here. That person is inside and simply lacks a permission; mixing the two would hide outsiders among legitimate people.

v12.39 Security

Deactivating a user now actually locks them out

If your installation was upgraded from an older version, switching someone off in Manage Users may not have been stopping them. The screen said "deactivated"; the server let them in anyway.

The check looked them up in the current user list and, finding them switched off, carried on to the old list the app was migrated from — and that old list still has everybody in it. So the "off" switch was read, and then quietly overruled by a leftover.

Now the current user list decides, both ways: switched on, they get their role; switched off, they are out, and the old list is not consulted. Somebody who was never added to the current list still gets in exactly as before, which is the one case the old list is there for.

This is worth checking on your own installation today: open Manage Users and confirm everyone switched off is someone you meant to switch off.

v12.38 Safety

The app now tells you when it has lost the connection

Until today the only sign was a small coloured dot in the corner, and you had to hover over it to read why. Nobody hovers over a dot. You keep working, you keep typing — and none of it is being saved.

Now a red bar across the top says you are offline, nothing you type can be saved, and the app is still retrying on its own. It cannot be dismissed: while it is still true, the warning is still true. When the connection comes back it turns green, says Back online, and disappears on its own after five seconds — the opposite rule, because by then it has stopped being news.

Opening the app never shows it: starting up is not a recovery, and greeting you with "Back online" would be reporting an outage that never happened.

v12.37 Fix

The Save button no longer scrolls out of the window

In a form taller than the screen — Add Expected Material, Entry, Exit — the buttons at the bottom sat at the end of the scrolling content. You had to scroll down to reach Save, and on a short screen the button was sliced in half by the edge of the window.

The row of buttons is now pinned to the bottom of the window, the same way the title and the ✕ have been pinned to the top for months. The form scrolls underneath it; the buttons stay.

And this matters more than convenience: the error message lives in that row. "Material 1 needs a name" appears right beside Save. With the row off-screen you press Save, nothing happens, and the reason is exactly where you are not looking.

If the window fits on your screen, it now fits — the ceiling went from 82% to 90% of the screen height, which is what Add Expected Material needed to stop scrolling at all on a normal laptop.

And every button in that row is now the same size. A "Cancel" at 88 pixels beside a "Save" at 120 was two sizes in one row; they are all one size now, whatever class they were written with, and none of them can wrap onto a second line and shove the row around.

v12.36 Fix

Every published version now says which one it is

Publishing from the menu labelled every version the same way, so the list in Google's deployment dialog read as a column of identical names with no way to tell one from the next. Each version is now stamped with the app version, its build and the date it went out.

The deployment's own name deliberately stays the same, and that is not an oversight: it is the key the app uses to find its existing deployment and update it in place. Change it and the next publish would create a new deployment — which means a new address, with every bookmark in the warehouse pointing at the old one.

v12.35 Safety

Your categories and locations can no longer be wiped by a hiccup

The code that saves your lists — categories, suppliers, projects, locations — emptied the column first and wrote the new list second. Between those two steps sits every ordinary interruption Google can throw: a quota, a timeout, a passing error. Anything landing there left that list empty, with nothing to warn you. You would find out on opening a dropdown with nothing in it.

It now writes first and clears what is left over afterwards. If the write fails, your old list is still there. An out-of-date list is a small, visible problem; an empty one is a large, invisible one.

And a second fault, found while fixing the first. A location's name and its type live in two columns side by side and only mean anything together — and they were written in two separate operations. If the first one landed and the second did not, the names and the types slid out of line: one rack took another's type, a rack appeared archived, one stopped being archived. Nothing failed and nothing said so. They are now written as a single block, which for Google is one operation: the half-done state no longer exists.

v12.34 Fix

"Load more" no longer throws you to the bottom of the list

Pressing it brought the next 75 movements and then jumped the view past all of them, to the very last row. You lost the place you were reading and had to scroll back up to find it.

Measured: the row you were looking at moved about 5,000 pixels off the top of the screen. Now it stays exactly where it was and the new rows arrive underneath, which is the whole point of a "more" button.

And the other button that grows the same list got the same treatment. "Load Older History" was quietly resetting the table back to its first 75 rows — so anyone who had pressed "Load more" ten times lost all ten, and then had to press it ten times again to reach the older movements they had just asked for. A button that brings things cannot hide the things that were already there.

v12.33 Fix

After a real archive run, the report says how each sheet ended up

Running the archive for real finished correctly and reported every step — but the one line that says what the two sheets hold now was missing. The numbers were in the step-by-step, spread across it; the summary that puts them side by side only ever appeared in the rehearsal.

It could not appear: the two figures were worked out inside the rehearsal's own branch, so a real run never had them. They are now worked out for both — and on a real run they are read back from the sheets after writing, not carried over from what was about to be written. Once something has been written, "how many did I mean to write" and "how many are there" are two different questions, and the second is the one worth printing.

v12.32 Fix

"Open WMS App" could hand you someone else's address

On a spreadsheet copied from one that had already been published, Google answers the question "what is this app's address?" with the original file's address. The copy has its own, somewhere else. Following the one Google gives you lands on "Sorry, unable to open the file at this time", and nothing about that page suggests the link was the problem.

The app already knew this — it keeps the real address in a setting that wins over Google's guess, and the setup wizard has been reading it that way for months. The menu entry was the one place that never asked.

Now it asks. And Push Update Live writes the address down the moment it publishes, which is the only moment it is known for certain, so the menu is right from then on without anybody typing anything.

A second one came out of the same check. Signing in with Google sends you back to the app afterwards, and on a copy with nothing saved that return address was Google's guess too — so the sign-in could send people back to somebody else's installation, failing with an error that looks nothing like its cause. It now prefers the recorded address as well.

v12.31 Internal

A table of contents for the code, and a test that keeps it honest

Nothing in the app changed. This one is for whoever has to work on it — today that is one person, and one day it will not be.

The server file is 13,279 lines across 68 sections. It now opens with a numbered index of those sections, so finding something is reading a list instead of scrolling. The index is generated from the file's own section headings, not typed, and a test regenerates it and compares it line by line: rename, move, add or remove a section without updating the index and the test fails.

That part is the point. A hand-written index is one more list that has to agree with something else with nothing forcing it — the exact shape of bug this codebase has caught itself making twice. And an index that lies is worse than no index at all: no index makes you search, a wrong one makes you believe you already have.

v12.30 New

Archive the old movements now, instead of waiting for 3am

The job that moves old movements out of the main list runs once a night. That made every attempt to check on it cost twenty-four hours: press save, wait, look tomorrow. There is now a button that runs the real job on the spot — the same job, the same safety checks, not a practice copy — under Advanced → Archive old movements NOW.

It rehearses first and shows you the numbers: how many would move out, how many would come back in, how many would stay, and what each sheet would hold afterwards. Nothing is written until you answer yes. The report you get after it runs is the same one, in the past tense.

And the last 30 days are never archived again, whatever the cutoff says. With the cutoff at six or twelve months that was already true by arithmetic — but it was true by accident, because it depended on a setting. A setting can be wrong, and a cutoff that reads wrong can send every movement to the archive and leave the main list empty. Now it is a rule, and when it has to step in, the report says so out loud.

v12.29 Fix

Every nightly run now records which version of the app it was

This is the one that explains the last two weeks. The failure on 1 October left a stack trace, and the stack trace said the function that broke was at line 2,148, twenty-two lines above its own trigger. In the version that added the safety checks, that same function sits at line 4,371 and is about three hundred lines long.

So the safety checks were not in the code that ran. The nightly job had been executing an older copy of the script while the new one sat in the editor — which is why three fixes in a row appeared to change nothing. Nothing anywhere in the app could answer "but is this the new code?", so the question went unasked for two weeks.

Now every run writes it down twice: once when it starts, in case it breaks on the first line, and once when it finishes. The version also rides along in the error record. One short line, and the question can never go unanswered again.

And the rehearsal button now actually starts. It was shipped without declaring who it is, so the only thing it ever said was "not authenticated". A diagnostic that does not start is worse than no diagnostic: it costs a day to somebody who was counting on it.

v12.28 Fix

When the nightly job fails, the log now says where it got to

Three failures in a row left exactly the same line: "the number of columns in the data does not match the number of columns in the range. The data has 23, and the range, 20." That line does not say which sheet, or at which step, or how wide either sheet was. It is the shape of the failure without the failure.

The job now leaves a trail as it goes — how wide each sheet is against the width the rows need, what the cutoff is, how many rows go each way, which of the two writes it was attempting, and whether the first one had finished. When it throws, all of that goes into the error log along with the stack and its line numbers.

Putting the rows back, added in v12.27, is a seatbelt — it is not the fix. Losing rows must not become a thing the app routinely recovers from. This is what turns the next failure into something that can actually be diagnosed instead of guessed at.

One detail worth recording: the trail very nearly never arrived. The error log discards anything that is not an object, so the first version — which passed a plain string — would have been dropped silently, and we would have had the same useless line while believing it now said something.

v12.27 Fix

If the nightly job loses rows, it puts them back

Since v12.14 the job has counted the movements before and after writing, so it could tell when rows went missing. And all it did was say so — a line in the error log, an email, and the sheet left broken. That happened three times, and all three times it was found hours later and fixed by hand from a backup.

Noticing you have just lost a thousand rows and not putting them back is nearly worse than not noticing: everything needed to repair it is in memory, two lines away. Both sheets are still held exactly as they were before anything was touched. So now they go back — straight away, without asking.

This does not depend on knowing why it failed, and that is the point of writing it this way. The cause of the three incidents is still not established. A repair that only works once you understand the cause is not a safety net, it is a hope.

The email still goes out either way: a silent repair would hide the cause, and the cause still needs finding.

v12.27 Fix

Older history loads newest first, a batch at a time

Load Older History fetched the entire archived history in one go and then said "already loaded". With 56 rows that looks like the app can only ever load 56; with twenty thousand it would be a trip that never finishes.

It now brings 300 at a time, starting with the most recent and working back, and each press continues where the last one stopped. Only the rows in that batch are read, so it stays quick however much history there is.

And the real gap was not the batching — it was that the button never said whether it had finished. "Older History (56)" cannot tell "56 of 56" from "56 of 1,271", so you have to guess. Now it says "Load 300 more — 56 loaded, 1,215 older still there", or "All older history loaded" when there is nothing left.

v12.26 New

You can rehearse the nightly archive whenever you like

The job that tidies old movements runs at 3 in the morning with nobody watching, and writes what it did to a tab nobody opens. When it went wrong it was found hours later and by accident. A failure you can only observe at 3am is a failure you cannot investigate.

There is now 🌙 Test the nightly archive in the Advanced menu. It runs tonight's job in full — the same checks, the same counts, the same split — and stops just before the two writes. It is safe to press at any time on real data, and it tells you what tonight would do: how wide each sheet actually is against the width the rows need, how many movements are in each, what your cutoff is and what it counts as old, how many would move each way, what each check says, and the exact error if one happens.

The rehearsal lives inside the real job rather than being a copy of it. A copy would be a second version that always agrees with itself, which is how a broken safety net went unnoticed for ten days. And it is tested like anything else — including that it truly writes nothing, which caught a first version that quietly widened the sheets while claiming to be a rehearsal.

v12.25 Fix

An empty movement list now tells you which kind of empty it is

The alarm added in v12.14 could never have gone off. It was written to notice the one thing that cannot happen on its own — a warehouse with stock and an archive with no movements — and it looped over the stock list as if it were an array. It is not; it is a map keyed by material. The loop ran zero times and the answer was always no. Ten days with the net up and a hole in it.

That is the same shape as the backup snapshot that never worked: a protection written, assumed good, and never once executed. What the two have in common is not carelessness — neither had a test. A protection without a test is not a protection, it is an intention. This one now has a test that runs it, and the old version fails it.

And there are three kinds of empty, not two. The third is the one that caused the scare: the recent list is empty because the nightly job moved everything past your cutoff into the archived history. Nothing is lost — but the screen looked exactly like the disaster. Telling somebody their warehouse broke when they only need to press a button is nearly as bad as saying nothing when it really did break.

So the list now says which one it is: a filter found nothing, everything is in the archived history (with how many, and the button that brings them back), or the impossible one, which still says to stop and touch nothing. And the nightly job itself now writes a line and emails when it leaves the recent list empty — even though the counts add up, because they do add up in that case and that is exactly why nobody was told.

v12.24 Fix

The number changes when the app says it changed

Save a movement and the app said done — and then took a few more seconds to change the quantity, put the stock back, and move the badge from In Stock to All at Site. Measured with a stopwatch on a real save.

What it was doing was worse than waiting. After a save the app repainted from its own cache — and the cache is the picture from before the movement you just saved. So the figure that appeared "instantly" was the old one. That is worse than a loading skeleton: a skeleton looks like something loading, an old number looks like a number. There was nothing on screen to say it was not true yet.

The fix was not to redo the arithmetic in the browser. That was the obvious route and it was the wrong one: two sets of arithmetic can disagree, and then the figure flickers to a wrong value before correcting itself — worse than waiting, and it breaks the rule this app is built on.

The server already had the after figures. It walks its own stock picture movement by movement to check the save is allowed, so by the time it answers it knows exactly what you have — and it was throwing that away. Now it sends it. There are not two sets of arithmetic: it is the server's, quoted. The full picture is still fetched behind, quietly; this brings forward what you look at, it does not replace the truth.

And the badge comes free. Since v12.17 the status is decided from those numbers rather than stored beside them, so correcting the numbers corrects In Stock → All at Site on its own. That was the point of unifying the statuses, and this is the first place it pays.

v12.23 New

The spreadsheet behind the app looks like part of the product

Open the file itself and three tabs were properly designed — the welcome page and the two legal documents — while the other seventeen were raw spreadsheet dumps with a bold header row. The contrast did more harm than no styling at all, because it showed we knew how.

The tab colour now tells you whether it is safe to edit by hand, which is the most common support question there is and was written down nowhere. Navy for the pages you read, blue for the data the system lives in, grey for the sheets the app rebuilds, pale grey for the logs that are only ever added to. And each rebuilt sheet carries a note on its first cell: "Rebuilt by Acopio from MASTER_ARCHIVE_V3. Edits here are overwritten — change the movement instead." That one line saves somebody an afternoon of correcting a total that keeps coming back.

Headers are navy with white type and a frozen first row. The rebuilt sheets and the logs are protected — as a warning, not a lock, deliberately: the app writes to them with the same account, so a real lock would be a new way for it to fail at night. The warning stops a person and does not stop the code.

Nothing you have set is overwritten. Column widths, tab order and cell formats are only applied to sheets that are still empty — a template, or a fresh install. On a system with months of data the app sets the colours, the headers and the notes and leaves everything else exactly as you arranged it, and says which sheets it left alone rather than staying quiet about it.

It runs when the sheets are created, when a master template is prepared, and as part of Check this installation — so a system restored from a backup gets its look back, since a copy brings the data and loses the formatting.

v12.22 Fix

Renaming a material takes everything of its own with it

A material is known by its category and its name — and five other things were filed under that name: how many units come in a box, its average cost, anything reserved of it, its minimum, and whether it is watched for low stock. Renaming a material, moving it to another category or merging it rewrote every movement and left all five behind.

That does two kinds of damage, and the second is the worse one. You are left with an orphan line pointing at a material that no longer exists — visible, at least. But the renamed material has quietly lost its figures. The next delivery by the box does not get divided, so the cost per unit comes out twelve times too high and blends into that material's average from then on. Nothing says a word.

All five now move with the material, and what moved is written into the audit log. When you merge two materials that both have figures, the survivor keeps its own — the one people will go on using should not quietly take on the numbers of the one that disappeared — and the dropped ones are counted in the log rather than vanishing.

v12.22 Fix

The Stock Dashboard columns stop moving when you filter

Every filter shifted the whole table. Measured across the categories: Stock started 114px further along in Window than in Shower, and Location 97px, without changing anything but the filter.

It was not the filter — it was the contents. The browser shares out the width by what is inside, and what is inside changes completely between categories: Window's names are long and Mirror's are short, Shower's locations read MIRRORS/SHOWERS WAREHOUSE (234) and Window's read A3A (54). So any filter that changes the mixture moved the table.

Each column now declares its own width, the way Movements has since v12.11, and the widths are measured rather than chosen — drawn with the real worst cases and taken from there. Name stays elastic: it is the one that grows with the window, and it absorbs the 200px that Category frees up when you filter by one, so nothing to its right moves at all.

v12.21 Fix

Putting a movement back can no longer break your stock
