# Welcome email — template

Sent by hand, per customer, right after they've paid and you're ready to hand
over their copy. Fill in the bracketed parts, paste into Gmail, send from your
own address — not automated, on purpose (see docs/BACKLOG.md → the retention
discussion for why: this is the one touchpoint that should feel like a person,
not a system).

Two things this email is doing at once, deliberately:
1. Getting them to a working system in the next ten minutes.
2. Telling them, plainly, that you'll check in — so when you actually do (day
   3, day 7, or because the automated check-in flagged them to you), it lands
   as "he said he would," not as out of nowhere.

---

**Subject: Your [Company Name] warehouse system is ready**

Hi [First Name],

Here's your copy of Acopio, set up and ready for [Company Name]:

**[paste the /copy link here]**

It takes about ten minutes, once. Everything lives in your own Google
account — nobody else can see your inventory, including me.

**Three things before you click it:**

1. **Use the Google account you want to be the owner.** The first person to
   open the link and finish setup becomes the admin. If that should be
   someone else on your team, forward this to them instead.
2. **Partway through, Google will show a warning** that says it hasn't
   verified the app. That's expected — it just means this copy belongs to
   you, not a stranger's app asking for your data. Click **Advanced**, then
   **Go to (your project name)**, and keep going.
3. **If anything looks confusing or gets stuck — stop and email me.** Don't
   push through something that doesn't make sense; that's exactly the kind
   of thing that's a two-minute fix for me and an hour of guessing for you.

The full step-by-step, with a screenshot for the scary-looking warning, is
here if you want it: [link to CUSTOMER-SETUP.md, or a hosted copy of it]

**What happens next:** I'll check in with you over the first couple of
weeks to see how it's going — not to sell you anything, just to catch
anything that's confusing before it becomes a reason to give up on it. If
you get there faster and start using it right away, even better — reply
anytime with questions, and welcome aboard.

[Your name]
[phone, if you want to offer it]

---

## Notes for you, not part of the email

- **Send this the same day you hand over the link**, not before — an
  install link with no context sitting in an inbox for a week is the first
  way this fails.
- **The day-3 / day-7 automated check-in (v9.77) backs this up, it doesn't
  replace it.** If it emails you that someone's stuck, that's your cue to
  reach out personally — a short call, not another email — same as the
  "what happens next" paragraph above promises.
- If you end up sending this often enough that retyping it is real friction,
  a Gmail template (Settings → Advanced → Templates) turns it into two
  clicks. Not worth setting up for the first few customers.
