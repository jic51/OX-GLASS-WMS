# Welcome email — template

Sent by hand, per customer, right after they've paid and you're ready to hand
over their copy. Fill in the bracketed parts, paste into Gmail, send from your
own address — not automated, on purpose (see docs/BACKLOG.md → the retention
discussion for why: this is the one touchpoint that should feel like a person,
not a system).

**Four things this email does at once, deliberately:**

1. **Sounds glad they're here.** They just paid a stranger $500 to install
   software into their own Google account. A flat, procedural email is the
   first thing that makes that feel like a mistake.
2. **Says again what they bought.** Not a sales pitch — a reminder, on the day
   they start, of what the thing is for. People buy in week one and forget by
   week three what they were promised.
3. **Gets them to a working system in the next ten minutes.**
4. **Tells them you'll check in** — so when you actually do (day 3, day 7, or
   because the automated check-in flagged them), it lands as "he said he
   would," not as out of nowhere.

Keep that order. The setup steps are the middle, not the opening: nobody is
excited by an instruction list, and the first two lines decide whether the
rest gets read.

---

**Subject: Your [Company Name] warehouse system is ready**

Hi [First Name],

It's ready — and I'm glad you're the one using it. Here's your copy of Acopio,
set up for [Company Name]:

**[paste the /copy link here]**

It takes about ten minutes, once.

---

**What you've got**

A real warehouse system that lives in **your own Google account**. Nobody else
can see your inventory — including me. One line each:

- **Entries, exits, transfers, returns and waste** — every movement tied to a
  person, a job and a rack.
- **Stock by location**, so "where is it?" has an answer before anyone walks.
- **Costs** — what you paid, what a job consumed, what waste cost you.
- **Low-stock alerts** on the materials you choose to watch.
- **Photos and documents** attached to the movement they belong to.
- **A nightly backup** into your own Drive, automatically.

---

**Three things before you click the link**

1. **Use the Google account that should own this.** The first person to open
   the link and finish setup becomes the admin. If that should be someone else
   on your team, forward this to them instead.
2. **Partway through, Google will warn you that the app isn't verified.**
   That's expected — it means this copy belongs to *you*, not to a stranger's
   app asking for your data. Click **Advanced**, then **Go to (your project
   name)**, and keep going.
3. **If anything looks confusing or gets stuck, stop and email me.** Don't
   push through something that doesn't make sense. That's a two-minute fix for
   me and an hour of guessing for you.

## Notes for you, not part of the email

- **Send this the same day you hand over the link**, not before — an install
  link with no context sitting in an inbox for a week is the first way this
  fails.
- **Don't list the whole backlog** under "What's coming." Two or three things
  actually in progress. A long list reads as a product that isn't finished,
  and every item on it becomes something they are now waiting for. Today that
  would honestly be: units per box/pallet so you can price by the case;
  templates for other kinds of warehouse; and a one-click low-stock email.
- **Never put a date on anything you haven't built.** The Terms say we don't
  commit to features or timelines unless agreed in writing, and an
  enthusiastic email is exactly where that gets accidentally undone.
- **"I answer within one business day" has to match the Terms.** It is section
  5, and it is the line they will remember and quote back. If you ever change
  the commitment there, change it here the same day.
- **The three links** are the ones the customer needs again later: setup,
  Terms, Privacy. Once acopio.net is up they can point at public pages; until
  then, attach the files or point at the tabs inside their own spreadsheet.
- **The day-3 / day-7 automated check-in (v9.77) backs this up, it doesn't
  replace it.** If it emails you that someone's stuck, that's your cue to
  reach out personally — a short call, not another email — same as the "what
  happens next" paragraph above promises.
- If you end up sending this often enough that retyping it is real friction, a
  Gmail template (Settings → Advanced → Templates) turns it into two clicks.
  Not worth setting up for the first few customers.
